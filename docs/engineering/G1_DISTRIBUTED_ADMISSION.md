# G1 distributed download admission

Separate NOLOGIN admission roles, per-replica login credentials, shared PostgreSQL quota and peer-HMAC key. Global fixed-minute and per-peer limits (defaults 120/20), 16 active leases, 2 GiB conservative reservation budget, accounting for encrypted staging overhead. Validly shaped requests are admitted before JWT verification, artifact authorization or retrieval. Local transport-peer and concurrency limits remain mandatory for malformed requests. Database stores pseudonymous peer HMACs rather than raw addresses or bearer tokens; rate counters are bounded by the global limit and reset on the next active minute. The fixed window permits boundary bursts. Shared addresses behind NAT or a proxy may share the per-peer limit; do not trust client-supplied forwarding headers.

Durable leases are serialized through a private singleton row and released only after cleanup. A lost DB connection, crashed process or ambiguous commit never auto-expires a lease. Safe availability recovery requires a separately authorized incident operator to independently confirm worker termination AND removal of its resources, then reconcile the specific lease with a unique approval reference. Separate credentials, runbook and monitoring are required; the database cannot verify external process death. This intentionally trades availability for avoiding unsafe overcommit. Lease reconciliation is append-only. Dedicated filesystem quotas remain required, as DB reservations cannot guarantee physical capacity or control external writers. No automatic TTL reclaim, production rollout, distributed network saturation, proxy integration, or upstream DDoS protection is claimed. Synthetic tests exercise distinct real PostgreSQL logins and HTTP gate interception.
